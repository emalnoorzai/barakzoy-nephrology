How Your Kidneys Work

Understanding Renal Filtration & Blood Pressure

Each kidney contains roughly one million microscopic filtering units (nephrons). Healthy kidneys filter waste products like creatinine and urea while regulating fluid volume, sodium, potassium, and blood pressure hormones. When hypertension or diabetes strains these delicate filters, early nephrology care helps protect long-term kidney function.

Patient Questions Answered

Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Find quick answers about appointments, insurance, lab work, dialysis, and kidney care.

1. Does the practice accept new patients?

Yes, Dr. Barakzoy is actively accepting new patients for nephrology consultations, chronic kidney disease management, hypertension, and second opinions at our Orange Park office.

2. Do I need a referral?

If you have Traditional Medicare or a PPO insurance plan, you typically do not need a referral to schedule an appointment. If you have an HMO plan or Tricare Prime, your primary care provider must submit a referral before your visit.

3. What should I bring to my appointment?

Please bring a valid photo ID, your current insurance cards, a complete list of your medications (or your medication bottles), any recent lab results or kidney ultrasounds not sent by your doctor, and your copayment.

4. How long are appointments?

New patient consultations generally take 30 to 45 minutes so Dr. Barakzoy can thoroughly review your medical history, labs, and imaging. Established follow-up visits typically take 15 to 20 minutes.

5. Which insurance plans are accepted?

We accept Medicare, Medicare Advantage plans, Florida Medicaid plans, Florida Blue (BCBS), Aetna, Cigna, UnitedHealthcare, Humana, WellCare, Ambetter, AvMed, and Tricare. Please call our office to confirm your specific plan network.

6. Does the practice accept Medicare and Medicaid?

Yes, we accept Traditional Medicare Part B, most Medicare Advantage plans, and Florida Medicaid / Medicaid Managed Care plans.

7. What does a nephrologist treat?

A nephrologist is a medical specialist trained in diagnosing and treating kidney diseases, high blood pressure (hypertension), fluid and electrolyte imbalances, protein or blood in the urine, diabetic kidney disease, dialysis care, and kidney transplant follow-up.

8. What does a low eGFR mean?

Estimated Glomerular Filtration Rate (eGFR) measures how well your kidneys filter waste from your blood. A lower eGFR indicates reduced kidney filtering capacity, which Dr. Barakzoy evaluates alongside urine protein, blood pressure, and trends over time to protect your remaining kidney function.

9. Does the practice treat dialysis patients?

Yes. Dr. Barakzoy manages patients on both hemodialysis and peritoneal dialysis and rounds regularly at Fresenius Kidney Care and DaVita Dialysis Centers throughout Orange Park, Clay County, and Jacksonville.

10. Does the practice manage transplant patients?

Yes, we evaluate patients for pre-transplant nephrology referrals and provide long-term post-transplant medical and blood pressure follow-up in coordination with regional transplant centers.

11. Do I need laboratory work before my appointment?

If you have had blood or urine tests within the past 3 months, we will review those results. If updated labs are needed prior to your consultation, our staff will notify you when confirming your appointment.

12. How do I request medical records?

You can download and complete our Medical Records Release Authorization form in the Forms section below and fax it to (904) 375-8568, or request records directly through your eCW Patient Portal.

13. How do I request prescription refills?

For the fastest turnaround, please contact your pharmacy directly and ask them to send an electronic refill request to our office, or submit a refill request through the eCW Patient Portal at least 3–5 business days before running out.

14. How do I contact the office about medical questions?

During business hours (Monday–Friday, 8:00 AM – 5:00 PM), please call (904) 579-3578 to speak with our clinical staff, or send a non-urgent message via the eCW Patient Portal. For medical emergencies, always dial 911.
